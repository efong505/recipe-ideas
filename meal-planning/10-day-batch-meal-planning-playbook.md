# Repeatable 10-day batch-meal planning playbook

**Purpose:** Build delicious, healthy, easy, affordable meals for two adults over 10 days, using a Power Pressure Cooker XL wherever practical, with a **$120 maximum total grocery budget**.

## Acceptance criteria

- Five **different, approved** main dishes.
- Approximately **8 substantial servings per dish** (two adults × lunch and dinner × two days); adjust to actual appetite and observed yield.
- **Every main dish includes meat, poultry or seafood.** Beans and lentils can stretch protein but are not the main protein.
- Total purchased groceries (including needed breakfasts, fruit and other essentials) **≤ $120**; preserve a buffer if possible.
- No recipes repeated simply by renaming the same base sauce or seasoning blend.
- One consolidated list, using home inventory first and date/store-specific prices.
- Recipe instructions match cooker capacity and safe cooking methods.
- Store food safely, with a two-day rotation or frozen surplus.

## Stage 1 — Discover flavors, then select

1. Generate **15–20 distinct options** across Southwest/New Mexican, Italian, Mediterranean, Cajun, Asian-inspired and comfort-food styles.
2. For each: name, short description, protein, flavor profile, rough 8-serving cost, cooking method/time, and freezer/reheat suitability.
3. Do not silently select the final five. Have the household approve or modify them.
4. Apply a **taste veto**. Previously rejected: peanut/chickpea/lentil vegetable stew. Exclude all vegan/vegetarian mains in future runs.
5. Prefer affordable meat sources, but do not require an identical protein in every meal.

## Stage 2 — Inventory before shopping

Ask for current refrigerator, freezer and pantry quantities, without assuming last month's inventory persists. Track:

| Ingredient | On hand | Needed across five dishes | Buy | Unit price | Extended price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Example: chicken quarters | 0 lb | 7 lb | 10 lb bag | $8.00 | $8.00 |

Do the same for breakfast supplies (eggs, oats, milk, bread, fruit), seasonings, oil and side dishes. Note whether packages contain edible cooked meat or bones; 10 lb bone-in chicken is not 10 lb boneless servings.

## Stage 3 — Build a price-checked cart

- Use **the user's actual chosen store**, loyalty-card tags, weekly deals and reduced-price stickers.
- Distinguish **confirmed receipt**, **shelf-tag price**, **online store price**, and **planning estimate**.
- Calculate: **total basket = sum(quantity bought × price)**. A recipe's allocated ingredient value is not new spending.
- Record taxes/fees only where applicable, and do not assume all food is taxed.
- Do not double count a marked-down item if the receipt already includes it.
- Target a buffer for unavailable stock or unexpected higher prices.
- If the total exceeds $120, revise ingredients and portion sizes *before* checkout, retaining adequate protein and vegetables.

## Stage 4 — Validate the five recipes

For each approved recipe include exact ingredient weights/volumes, servings, prep time, pressure and natural-release time, temperature where applicable, numbered steps, quantities from shared packages, optional substitutions, leftovers and nutritional estimates **clearly labeled estimates**.

**Cooker checks:**

- Verify the specific Power Pressure Cooker XL manual and capacity; not all models have identical settings.
- Respect maximum fill lines; leave headroom for swelling/foaming ingredients, especially rice/beans/lentils.
- Cook bone-in chicken completely (165°F at the thickest safe measurement point) and account for bone removal.
- For pasta/oatmeal, follow the model manual; use an uncovered simmer if pressure cooking is restricted.
- Add milk/cheese after releasing pressure; avoid scorching dense tomato sauces and peanut butter on the bottom.
- If the batch is too large, split it or stage components safely; never force a single pressure cycle.

## Stage 5 — Put the cooking calendar together

| Days | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Cook batch A; eat lunch and dinner from planned portions |
| 3–4 | Cook batch B |
| 5–6 | Cook batch C |
| 7–8 | Cook batch D |
| 9–10 | Cook batch E |

This is a framework, not a rigid requirement to eat the same dish four consecutive times. Freeze portions and interchange meals if appetite or taste calls for variety. Plan inexpensive breakfasts separately.

## Stage 6 — Reconcile actual spending and outcomes

After shopping, reconcile receipt lines to the proposed list and update the plan to reflect *what was actually bought*. After cooking, record:

- Purchase date/store, checkout amount, budget remaining.
- Ingredient substitutions, package sizes, and unused ingredients.
- Actual serving yield and how many leftovers were consumed/frozen.
- Taste feedback per dish: **repeat / modify / avoid** (plus short reason).
- Time spent cooking/cleanup and any pressure-cooker constraints.
- Any discarded food, safety issues, or missed budget assumptions.

**Success means:** affordable, genuinely enjoyed meals, safe and realistic pressure-cooker preparation, minimal waste, and an accurate record for future replication—not simply producing five cheap recipe titles.
