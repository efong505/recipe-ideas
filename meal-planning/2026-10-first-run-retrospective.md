# First-run retrospective: 10 days, five pressure-cooker batches, $120 ceiling

**Planning date:** October 30, 2026  
**Intended meal period:** October 31–November 9, 2026 (10 days)  
**Household:** two adults  
**Primary cooker:** Power Pressure Cooker XL  
**Store:** Smith's Food & Drug, Albuquerque, New Mexico  
**Budget ceiling:** $120 for groceries, including inexpensive breakfasts, lunches and dinners  
**Status:** Household reported the plan was successful overall, with one recipe rejected on taste.

## Five planned dishes in the first run

1. **New Mexico Green Chile Chicken & Potato Stew** — chicken quarters stretched with potatoes, beans, vegetables, and chile.
2. **Southwest Turkey, Sweet Potato & Lentil Chili** — 1 lb discounted ground turkey stretched with lentils, beans, sweet potato, and tomatoes. **Explicit positive taste feedback.**
3. **Creamy Italian Chicken & Broccoli Pasta** — chicken, whole-grain pasta, broccoli, tomato, and modest amounts of milk/cheese. Later referenced as cooked with leftovers.
4. **Savory Peanut, Chickpea & Lentil Vegetable Stew** — inexpensive plant-based batch. **Taste failure; exclude from repeat.**
5. **Smoky Salsa Chicken, Rice & Bean Bowls** — shredded chicken, rice, beans, salsa, vegetables, and cheddar.

The above names capture the plan as discussed. They are **not** evidence that every batch yielded exactly eight measured portions or that every individual dish received a separate favorable review. Preserve those distinctions when evaluating future runs.

## Starting pantry advantage

At the store, the household reported: about **27 eggs**, **3¾ lb potatoes**, **¾ gallon milk**, peanut butter, a large bag of oats, bread, and carrots already on hand. This inventory check stopped unnecessary repurchases of major breakfast and recipe staples. An additional 18 eggs brought the potential egg inventory to about 45 eggs.

## What made this successful

1. **A firm, all-food ceiling.** The goal was not an arbitrary dinner-only estimate; breakfasts, lunch leftovers and fruit also counted.
2. **Batch cooking rather than daily cooking.** Five recipes made approximately two days of lunches/dinners each; less time spent prepping and cleaning.
3. **Pantry-first planning.** Inventory was gathered before the final shopping list; groceries on hand reduced the cash needed.
4. **Buying protein opportunistically.** Low-priced leg quarters and a marked-down pound of turkey anchored several high-protein dishes.
5. **Extending meat with supportive ingredients.** Potatoes, beans, lentils, vegetables, rice and pasta increased portions without making every dish meatless.
6. **Sharing ingredients without repeating flavors.** Green chile, Southwest chili, Italian pasta and smoky salsa bowls tasted different while using overlapping groceries.
7. **Store-realistic pricing.** Prices were checked against Smith's shelf tags, markdowns and receipt, not solely generalized estimates.
8. **Iterating during shopping.** The plan changed when the household reported existing eggs, potatoes, milk, oats and the actual turkey quantity.
9. **Leftovers planned at the outset.** Serving targets, refrigeration and reheating were designed into the meal plan.
10. **Taste feedback mattered.** The rejected peanut-lentil dish is a meaningful outcome: being cheap and nutrient-dense is not enough if the family will not eat it.

## Corrections to preserve for next time

- **No vegan or vegetarian main dishes.** Beans/lentils are fine as supporting ingredients in meat-containing dishes (e.g., successful turkey chili), but do not propose a meatless main or repeat the peanut-lentil stew.
- **Choose meals before building a cart.** First offer 15–20 distinct meat/poultry/seafood recipes; have the household approve five; then request current inventory and current prices.
- **Avoid big-pot assumptions.** Eight servings can exceed a six-quart cooker's safe fill. Use staged cooking, split batches, or cook side components separately; give realistic total cooking and release times.
- **Use exact ingredient math.** Track quantity purchased, on hand, allocated per recipe, and unallocated; do not promise more chicken/beans/sweet potatoes than exists.
- **Respect cooking-method restrictions.** Consult the specific Power Pressure Cooker XL model's manual; cook pasta or oatmeal uncovered rather than assuming they can be pressure-cooked, and add dairy/cheese after pressure cooking.
- **Refrigerate promptly and freeze surplus.** Poultry must reach **165°F**; cooked leftovers typically refrigerate **3–4 days** and reheat to **165°F**. Refrigerate perishables within **2 hours**.
- **Do not double count discounts.** For reduced-price sticker items, distinguish the unit price/photo from whether the item appeared on the receipt.
- **Keep cost language calibrated.** A printed receipt is a confirmed transaction; shelf tags may depend on membership; online prices and per-serving costs are estimates.
- **Document the evaluation.** After each run, record taste rating, actual portions, leftover waste, substitution changes, actual spending, and foods to repeat/avoid.
