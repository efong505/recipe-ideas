# Reusable prompt: 10-day, $120, pressure-cooker batch meal plan

Copy everything below into a new Recipes conversation when starting a new shopping period.

---

I want a **new 10-day affordable, healthy and flavorful meal plan for two adults**. Use the proven approach from our first October–November 2026 run, documented in the recipe-ideas repository under `meal-planning/`.

**Goal:** Spend at most **$120 total on groceries** for ten days (not just dinners), preferably with a cushion, while eating filling, well-seasoned meals and cooking fewer days.

**Equipment:** We have a **Power Pressure Cooker XL**; favor one-pot or mostly-one-pot meals. Follow safe pressure-cooker methods and realistic timings for the specific model. Eight-serving batches may require staged cooking or smaller separate batches.

**Batch strategy:** Choose **five different main recipes**. Each should target about **eight substantial servings** so that two adults can eat lunch and dinner from the batch for approximately two days. Reheating, refrigeration, and freezing matter. Include easy and cheap breakfasts, fruit, and other essentials in the final budget.

**Flavor and family preference:**
- Every main dish must contain **meat, poultry or seafood**. **No vegan or vegetarian main recipes.**
- Legumes, potatoes, rice, vegetables and pasta can stretch meat economically and improve nutrition.
- **Do not repeat the peanut, chickpea and lentil vegetable stew**—it was disliked.
- The first round's successful flavor direction included Southwest Turkey, Sweet Potato & Lentil Chili; Creamy Italian Chicken & Broccoli Pasta; New Mexico Green Chile Chicken & Potato Stew; and Smoky Salsa Chicken, Rice & Bean Bowls.
- For this round, **change the menu**, not merely the names: suggest Southwest/New Mexican, Italian/Mediterranean, Cajun/Creole, Asian-inspired, comfort food and other creative dishes. Balance bold flavor with nutritional quality.

**Work in stages, and stop for our approval at each major decision:**

**Stage 1 — Options only.** Give me **15–20 different appealing recipe ideas** organized by cuisine. For each provide a short appetizing description, main protein, defining ingredients, flavor profile, approximate *eight-serving* ingredient-use cost, estimated cooking time, Pressure Cooker XL suitability, and reheating/freezer quality. Make clear these costs are estimates. Do **not** choose the final five or issue a shopping list yet. We will choose and revise.

**Stage 2 — Pantry check.** After we choose five, request our **current** pantry, refrigerator, freezer and seasonings inventory, and exact cooker size if needed. Do not assume last month's on-hand food remains.

**Stage 3 — Store-level budget.** Use our actual intended grocery store (often Smith's Food & Drug in Albuquerque, New Mexico), current shelf/sale/card prices when obtainable, and realistic package sizes. Build **one consolidated list** rather than separate duplicated ingredient purchases. Show a clear distinction between confirmed pricing and estimates. Include breakfast ingredients, sides, snacks/fruit, and a remaining buffer under $120. Do not double count pantry inventory or receipt entries. Offer cost-saving alternatives for us to approve.

**Stage 4 — Full recipes after approval.** Write all five recipes with exact quantities, 8-serving target, step-by-step Power Pressure Cooker XL directions, safe times and release procedure, temperature checks, how to avoid overfilling, substitutions, storage/reheating, estimated cost per batch/per serving, and *estimated* per-serving calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber and sodium. Cook pasta uncovered when the appliance manual restricts pressurized pasta; add dairy after pressure release. Do not invent a recipe image or PDF unless requested.

**Stage 5 — Ten-day rotation.** Put selected dishes on a practical cooking calendar with approximately five sessions, leftover lunches, simple breakfasts, variety options for day-two leftovers, and a safe refrigerate/freeze plan.

**Stage 6 — Checkout and retrospective.** When I share actual shelf prices or a receipt, update the ingredient ledger and total using **actual purchases**. If an item was separately purchased or ambiguously visible, state the uncertainty rather than double count it. After we eat the meals, ask for flavor feedback and document what to repeat, alter, or avoid in the next round.

Do not substitute a bland recipe for a delicious one just to hit a cost target. The first run succeeded because it combined **flavor, a realistic $120 ceiling, large-batch cooking, pantry reuse, store deals, and real household feedback**.

**Start with Stage 1: present the options for us to review. Do not finalize the plan before we select five dishes.**
